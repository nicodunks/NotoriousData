The stance · an independent assessment

Karate to boxer, checked joint by joint

The popular story has six or seven parts: a wide karate stance that narrowed, toes that went flat, a bounce that stopped, a torso that tipped forward, shoulders that hunched, weight that came onto the front foot. Each part maps onto something the skeleton can measure. Every standing frame in fifteen fights, ten a second, his opponent measured in the same frames as the camera control, and each trend re-checked using only side-on shots, where a 2-D picture of a stance is most honest.

Each faint figure is a real three-second stretch of McGregor standing in southpaw, playing at real speed, centred on where his hips were in that stretch. Their motion is his bounce and drift. The bold figure is the median of all of them at each moment, so it moves with them; because the stretches aren't in step, it keeps only the motion they share.

The median 2012–2015 stance turning into the median 2016–2021 stance. At 1× this is the true difference: small. Exaggerating multiplies the difference so its direction is visible: the torso tips forward, the head comes out over the front, the hips settle back. The feet barely move.

Counter-striker or pressure fighter

Who started it?

The other half of the story: that he began as a patient counter-striker and became a forward-walking head hunter. Every hand strike in every upright second, both fighters, sorted by what came just before it. A counter answers an opponent attack within 0.7 s; a first strike comes with no opponent attack in the 1.5 s before.

Neither half of the story holds. He was a first striker from the start: about seven in ten of his strikes began an exchange, in 2012 and in 2021. Counters stayed near a fifth. His share of strikes at head height stayed near a half. His time spent advancing didn't rise, and in most fights his opponent moved forward more than he did.

The one change runs the other way. Compared with the men in front of him, more of his late-career strikes came while he was moving backward (relative trend ρ +0.66, p 0.03): the opponents of 2016 to 2021 did that less, he did it a little more. The pull counter, above, is part of that.

Who started an exchange is read from timing alone and doesn't depend on the camera. "Moving forward" is read from his hips on screen, and a panning camera blurs who moved; it's measured identically for both fighters, but treat it as the weaker of the two.
