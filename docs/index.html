Which Conor fight is this?

Ten fights, one exchange each. McGregor in red.

We let a model from behavioural neuroscience (keypoint-MoSeq) split his movement into short, repeated pieces, "syllables", from the joints alone, knowing nothing about fighting.

  • Eight syllables cover nine-tenths of his time on his feet: the long stance, tall and light, stepping in, lunge and throw.
  • The same vocabulary in 2012 and 2021, in about the same proportions.

The long stance

The footage
Fifteen fights, 2012 to 2021: three Cage Warriors title fights and twelve UFC fights, from official UFC, Fight Pass and broadcaster uploads (two fan uploads where no official one exists). Only live action counts: the fight clock is detected on screen in every frame, so walkouts, replays, slow motion and breaks drop out. The Aldo fight survives only as a replay package and is left out.
Finding the fighters
A YOLOv8 detector fine-tuned on UFC footage finds both men in each frame, ignoring the referee and crowd. Who is who comes from the colour of their shorts, held through clinches by following each body from frame to frame.
The skeletons
RTMW-x, a whole-body pose model (via rtmlib), places 133 points on each fighter: body, feet, hands and face. It runs every frame at the broadcast's own rate (30 a second). Points near the frame edge or below a confidence threshold are treated as unseen; nothing is drawn or measured that the model didn't see.
Units
Centimetres come from McGregor's height (175 cm): his leg is about 49 % of it, which sets his torso at about 47 cm. Speeds are screen speeds: the fist's peak speed in the broadcast picture, in each fighter's own torso lengths a second. They are not real-world speeds, but both men are measured the same way.
Punches, steps and counters
A punch is the arm opening fast toward the opponent; straight or hook from the shape of the fist's path (about three in four detections confirmed as committed punches by eye). A step is one foot moving against the other, so camera pans can't fake one. A pull or slip is the head moving back or down at least 5 cm against the hips while a punch comes in; a counter is a punch of his own within 0.6 s.
The comparison and the bar
Every number is McGregor against the man in front of him, in the same frames and under the same camera, with zoom, viewing angle and distance regressed out. Each fight counts once. A finding must survive Holm correction across everything tested in its family and be at least 0.4 of the normal moment-to-moment spread; others are labelled as patterns.
The fade
Every half-minute of standing footage in his long fights, placed on the fight clock, fitted with a flat-then-declining line whose starting minute the data chooses. Whole fights are resampled 2,000 times to see how stable that minute is.
Syllables
keypoint-MoSeq (Weinreb et al., 2024), a model from behavioural neuroscience, fitted to 17 body points on side-on footage only. The map is a t-SNE of each occurrence's model state; names were given by watching the clips.
Limits
Fifteen fights is not many, and a broadcast is a flat picture: movement toward the camera is invisible and whether a punch landed can't be measured. Everything was checked by eye on samples, not hand-labelled. Full rules and every test are in the code.
